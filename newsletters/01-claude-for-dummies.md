## 1. What is Claude?

Claude is an AI you talk to. You type something, it types back. It can write, think, summarize, analyze documents, work with your files, and help you make decisions. It's made by a company called Anthropic.

If you've used ChatGPT before, Claude is the same kind of thing. If you've never used any AI, Claude is a great place to start.

### How it actually works (in 30 seconds)

**1 - Auto-complete at scale.** Claude predicts the next word, billions of times per response. It's pattern-matching at a speed that feels like thinking, but it's not thinking the way you do. This is why Claude sounds confident even when it's wrong.

**2 - Sycophancy.** Claude is trained to be helpful and agree with you. Side effect: if you say something false, Claude might nod along instead of correcting you. Don't trust agreement. You decide directions.

**3 - Tokens.** Claude reads and writes in chunks called tokens. A token is roughly a word. Every conversation has a limit on how many tokens fit. This is why long conversations eventually become too much: the memory fills up.

> **Remember.** Claude is an auto-complete machine at superhuman scale. It sounds sure of itself because it's made to agree with you. It uses tokens to understand and memorize your conversation until it can't anymore.

## 2. Claude vs. ChatGPT

Claude and ChatGPT are the same species. You talk to both the same way. You can ask both the same questions. But they have different personalities and strengths.

**Where Claude wins:**

- **Writing voice.** Claude's default writing is less AI-flavored than ChatGPT's.
- **Long documents.** Claude can read a 200-page document in one go without losing track.
- **File work.** Claude's desktop app can see your local folder and work with your files directly.
- **Multi-step jobs.** Claude has Cowork mode that runs tasks for minutes to hours.

**Where ChatGPT still wins:**

- **Voice mode.** ChatGPT's voice experience is infinitely better right now.
- **Image generation.** Use ChatGPT or Gemini. Claude doesn't generate images.
- **Search.** ChatGPT with search feels faster than Claude with search.
- **Research.** The extended-thinking version of ChatGPT is better suited for research.
- **Multilingual.** ChatGPT is better than Claude. Gemini is probably even better.

> **Tip.** You don't have to pick one. Most people use both for different jobs. Claude for writing and long work. ChatGPT for voice, images, and quick search. Grok and ChatGPT for extended research. Gemini for images.

**Bottom line:**

1. Both AIs are average by default. What you feed them matters more than which one you pick.
2. Both AIs are infinitely better when you pay for them.

## 4. The Three Claudes

### #1. Browser Claude (claude.ai)

You type, you get an answer. Works on the free plan. Best for quick drafts, summaries, thinking out loud. Closest experience to ChatGPT. If you're brand new, start here.

### #2. Desktop Claude (Mac or Windows app)

Same account as the browser, but installed on your computer. Why it matters: it can see your local files, and you unlock the three modes: Chat, Cowork, and Code.

### #3. Cowork (inside the desktop app)

The best thing to happen to AI since ChatGPT. It does real work for minutes to hours while you do something else. You give it a task, it plans the steps, reads your files, writes outputs, and asks you questions along the way. Paid plans only and desktop only.

> **Remember.** Browser = ask. Desktop = ask + access to your files. Cowork = ask + access + do the work while you get coffee.

## 5. How to talk to Claude

Five rules. A **prompt** is the text query you send to an AI.

**Rule 1: Be specific.** "Write me an email" is vague. "Write a follow-up email to a client named Sarah who missed our Tuesday call. Tone: friendly but firm. 4 sentences max." gets you something you can actually send.

**Rule 2: Give examples.** Single best thing you can do. Paste something you wrote that you liked. Tell Claude: "write like this." Claude learns faster from examples than from instructions.

**Rule 3: Say what you want, not what you don't want.** "Don't make it too formal" is weaker than "write it like a text to a colleague."

**Rule 4: Start short, add detail.** Start with 2 sentences. See what comes back. Then add: "make it shorter," "change the tone," "add a section about X."

**Rule 5: If it gets confused, start a new chat.** Long conversations get messy. A fresh start is free.

> **Warning.** Claude will always give you an answer. That doesn't mean the answer is right. Check anything. Treat Claude as a partner, not the single source of truth.

## 6. What Claude is good at

- **Writing.** First drafts, rewrites, editing, adapting to your voice if you give it examples.
- **Summarizing.** Drop a 50-page PDF, ask for a 1-page summary with page references.
- **Thinking with you.** Structuring a messy idea. Pressure-testing a decision.
- **Working with your files.** Desktop and Cowork can read your local folder & create files (PPT, XCL, DOCX).
- **Long documents.** Upload 200-page files, full context, nothing lost.
- **Reasoning step by step.** Give it a complex question. Ask it to think step by step.

---

## 7. What Claude is bad at

- **Real-time information.** Claude doesn't know what happened today unless it has a search tool connected.
- **Precise math.** Don't use Claude as a calculator for anything that matters, unless it's running code.
- **Vague prompts.** If you say "write something good," you'll get something generic.
- **Being a source of truth.** Claude sounds authoritative even when it's wrong. Always verify facts, dates, quotes, names.
- **Image generation.** Claude can read images but cannot create them. Use ChatGPT or Gemini.

> **Warning.** The most common beginner mistake: vague requests assuming Claude is some otherworld god. It's a very good employee — probably your best — that needs direction from a boss: you.

## 8. The 3 words that matter

### Token

The unit Claude thinks in. Every word you type, every file you upload, every response Claude gives gets chopped into tokens.

- Your usage limits are counted in tokens.
- A page of text is roughly 500 tokens.
- Long chats hit a ceiling because the context window is finite.

> **Remember.** A page is about 500 tokens. If Claude acts dumb after a long conversation, start a fresh chat.

### Cowork

Cowork is Claude running for minutes to hours on your computer, doing a real task while you do something else.

**Example.** You have a folder of 40 messy invoices. Different formats. Different fonts. Some PDFs, some screenshots. You want them cleaned up, grouped by client, with a drafted follow-up email for each overdue one.

- **With ChatGPT:** copy-paste each invoice, ask, get an answer, repeat 40 times. An afternoon.
- **With Cowork:** open the desktop app, point it at the folder, describe the task in one sentence, walk away. Come back 15 minutes later. The folder has cleaned files, a summary spreadsheet, and 40 drafted emails.

My Cowork folder has 3 subfolders:

- **about-me**: who you are, how you work, what you write.
- **outputs**: so I can easily find again what Claude did in the past.
- **templates**: my favorite work with Claude, so it can do it again easily.

### Claude Code

Claude running inside your terminal. Built for developers. If you don't code, you don't need it. Cowork does 80% of what Claude Code does for non-developers, with a visual interface.

Quick summary:

- **Token** = what you're paying for.
- **Cowork** = Claude working for you.
- **Claude Code** = Claude for devs.

## 9. Words you can ignore (for now)

- **Projects.** A folder inside Claude that remembers context across chats.
- **Artifacts.** The side pane where Claude opens documents, code, or mini-apps.
- **Skills.** Reusable prompts you trigger by name inside Cowork or Chat. Saved workflows. Example: /negotiation.
- **Connectors.** How Claude talks to Slack, Gmail, Google Drive, Granola, Notion.
- **MCP.** Technical infrastructure for connecting things to Claude. You'll never have to do anything yourself.
- **Plugins.** Bundles of Skills and Connectors for Cowork. Like a small app store.

## 10. 10 things to try in Claude your first week

1. Copy-paste 3 of your LinkedIn posts. Ask Claude to write 3 new ones in the same voice.
2. Upload a 50-page PDF. Ask for a 1-page summary with page references.
3. Upload your last 5 meeting notes. Ask for a decisions log.
4. Point Cowork at your Downloads folder. Ask what's in there and what you could safely delete.
5. Paste a messy email thread. Ask for the 3 next actions and who owes what.
6. Connect your Gmail to Claude. Do the messy email task again, no pasting needed.
7. Give it a Google Doc you've been meaning to edit. Ask for a sharper rewrite with a diff.
8. Feed it a spreadsheet. Ask what patterns it notices.
9. Paste a competitor's pricing page. Ask how yours compares and what's missing.
10. Give it your calendar for the week. Ask what to decline, and why.

> **Tip.** Keep a note file called **prompts-that-worked**. Every time a prompt gives you something useful, save it. In 2 weeks you'll have a personal library worth more than any prompt pack you could buy.
